1. What is Kira World?
Slides 4-5
Kira World is our developing platform for persistent synthetic identities and digital environments. Its architecture goal separates shared machinery from individual histories, experiences and choices. A portable review/runtime lane and World Shell exist. World Builder authoring is paused, and complete live world and embodiment integration remains unfinished.
Follow-up: Describe one boundary, such as whose observation a record represents, rather than promising every planned capability.

2. What is NewBrain today?
Slides 11-13,24
NewBrain is an early research family with separate fitted-cell, synthetic-circuit and learning experiments. Its tiny standalone language extension learns from scratch without Qwen inference. Review retained old and new lessons at 100% across five fresh seeds on the same exposed task. It is not connected to the original spiking circuit or Kira conversation. A separate one-seed pixel learner failed to generalize.
Follow-up: Keep learned language, visual learning, fixed circuit dynamics and ordinary stored records distinct.

3. Is NewBrain better than Qwen?
Slides 11,13
The scratch review model and Qwen each answered 16/16 on the same narrow command subset, which only used red cubes. This does not establish general equality. The scratch learner has a 25-entry vocabulary and no open conversation. A handwritten grammar also scored 100% on the full toy task. The earlier association experiment tied indexed memory.
Follow-up: Specify the task and preserve different training histories and timing boundaries.

4. Does NewBrain use less RAM?
Slides 13,24
The language training Python process peaked at 40.30 MiB, including runtime, data and multiple models. Its arrays occupied 37,144 bytes, a different metric. The fixed-circuit run had a 345.43 MiB cumulative known-family peak. Neither is a same-task whole-application RAM comparison with Qwen.
Follow-up: Parameter bytes, process lifetime peak, family peak and GPU memory measure different things.

5. What does the review experiment show?
Slides 11
In the five-seed replication, every review run answered 216/216 old examples and 162/162 new examples correctly. Without review, old accuracy ranged from 78/216 to 105/216, or 36.11% to 48.61%. One no-review run also missed five of 54 new wording examples. Equal update counts still gave different old and new training exposure. The task was already exposed.
Follow-up: Review prevented forgetting in this controlled task. It does not prove human learning or general conversation.

6. What do the 24 body checks prove?
Slides 16,22
They cover earlier narrow software mechanics under their own synthetic conditions. They do not show that the current anatomical body can blink, breathe or move naturally. Its static rig and saved rest-state reopen now pass; movement qualification remains ahead.
Follow-up: Keep synthetic component controls separate from actual current-body observations.


7. Has NewBrain vision or hearing passed?
Slides 12,16
The synthetic visual learner passed ten code checks and memorized 96 training images. Accuracy fell to 27/72 with changed position, size and brightness, and to zero on unseen color/shape combinations. Colors stayed correct but shapes failed to generalize. Own learned hearing is not demonstrated. The local media baseline used external Whisper and Qwen.
Follow-up: The capable pixel rule scored 252/252. At confidence 0.70, the learner accepted 67 wrong withheld answers: 22 nuisance, 23 combination and 22 combined. Keep these adverse findings.

8. Would a realistic body make the brain better?
Slides 16,22
Functional feedback can change what a controller can sense and learn. A detailed body can also add cost without helping the task. We plan a matched simple-versus-detailed comparison when anatomy, rig and mechanics are ready.
Follow-up: Compare common inputs first, then additional feedback separately. Track body and neural cost.

9. Can Kira remember something Lisa experienced?
Slides 4-5
The intended design distinguishes shared information from personal experience. Lisa could receive Kira's report with its source attached. That should not become Lisa's own observation. Full application isolation needs testing across every access and recovery route.
Follow-up: Use invented identities on slide 5, not real private records.

10. Is this just a database with an LLM?
Slides 3,11-13
Ordinary files and databases serve some persistence roles. The new command learner has trainable embeddings, recurrent weights and output heads, trained by gradient updates from random weights. Its learned checkpoints survived saving and reloading. That is different from an episodic memory record, which remains a separate capability to test.
Follow-up: The command hidden state resets for each instruction. It does not remember a conversation.

11. How do you correct a wrong memory?
Slides 6,23
The teaching pattern adds a sourced correction linked to the earlier assertion and selects the corrected current view. The earlier assertion stays marked as superseded where retention is appropriate. Deletion is a separate operation with its own policy.
Follow-up: Check the saved result and reopening behavior, not just the reply.

12. How do you stop dreams or generated ideas becoming facts?
Slides 7
Store the origin explicitly and preserve it through retrieval and summarization. A dream, hypothesis or generated illustration should remain distinguishable from an observation. Dream and replay subsystems are future experiments here, not established subjective experiences.
Follow-up: A summary must preserve the source distinction. The slide artwork is conceptual, not anatomy data.

13. Is Kira conscious or does she have emotions?
Slides 15,25
We have no evidence of subjective experience in this software. Functional properties such as persistent state, source monitoring or emotion-like variables could be tested. Passing those tests would not settle consciousness.
Follow-up: Separate measurable mechanisms from a claim about inner experience.

14. Can you copy a person's mind or memories from a brain scan?
Slides 25
These sources and synthetic circuits do not establish that capability. A fitted cell or regional model is not someone's full wiring or autobiographical history. Unknown donor, region and wiring information stays unknown.
Follow-up: Do not use percentages of copied minds or guarantees of exact identity.

15. Why local processing?
Slides 3,15
It gives us control over where records live and makes some inspection and offline use possible. It also requires hardware, updates and operational care. Network services, logs, exports and backups are assessed separately.
Follow-up: Local operation alone is not a security certification.

16. Do you use cloud services or blockchain?
Slides 3,15
The research in this talk focuses on local computation and retained state. Any external service needs a declared role and data boundary. We do not claim that distributed deployment or blockchain integration is already built.
Follow-up: Decentralization of control and a particular ledger technology are different design questions.

17. How do you prevent repeated actions after a crash?
Slides 8-9
The proposed pattern retains an operation identifier before dispatch, then reconciles that identifier and its receipt on recovery. An uncertain attempt stays uncertain. Each implementation needs qualification; the pattern does not prove every operation safe.
Follow-up: Distinguish a missing response from an actual failed operation.
