# Expanded visual AI history - image and fact sources

This source index accompanies the **42-page** [AI History and NewBrain PDF](AI-History-and-NewBrain.pdf), updated 2026-10-10.

## ELIZA screenshot and rights

The illustrated ELIZA conversation is a screenshot from Anthony C. Hay and Max Hay's **2023 reimplementation** using the historical DOCTOR script: [original image](https://github.com/anthay/ELIZA/blob/master/doc/action-shot.jpg), [implementation](https://github.com/anthay/ELIZA). The screenshot identifies the work as **CC0 1.0 Public Domain**. It is not an archival screenshot of a 1966 computer. The image is included in [this folder](images/ELIZA-DOCTOR-reimplementation-2023.jpg) to preserve its attribution and let readers view it separately.

The original source is Joseph Weizenbaum, [ELIZA - A Computer Program for the Study of Natural Language Communication Between Man and Machine](https://doi.org/10.1145/365153.365168), *Communications of the ACM* **9**(1), 36-45 (1966). See also the historical [DOCTOR script](https://findingeliza.org/doctor.html) and the [ELIZA effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELIZA_effect). Perceived understanding is not proof of understanding.

## Sources for added historical milestones

- **Babbage / Lovelace, 1830s-1843:** [Science Museum Collection](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp38764/ada-lovelace).
- **Turing's imitation game, 1950:** [Computing Machinery and Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433).
- **Dartmouth project, proposed 1955 / convened 1956:** [McCarthy et al. proposal](https://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/dartmouth/dartmouth.html).
- **Logic Theorist, General Problem Solver:** [Carnegie Mellon history](https://www.cmu.edu/simon/what-is-simon/history.html); [Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/ai-and-play-part-1-how-games-have-driven-two-schools-of-ai-research/).
- **Shakey, 1966-72:** [SRI International history](https://www.sri.com/case-study/the-man-the-myth-the-legend-meet-shakey-the-robot-the-worlds-first-ai-based-robot/).
- **PARRY, 1972:** research program by Kenneth Colby.
- **MYCIN, 1970s:** [Edward Shortliffe on MYCIN](https://doi.org/10.1145/1408800.1408906).
- **XCON, 1980:** [R1 and Beyond - AI Technology Transfer at DEC](https://doi.org/10.1609/aimag.v5i4.460).
- **Deep Blue (1997), Watson (2011):** [IBM game AI history](https://www.ibm.com/history/early-games); [Watson history](https://www.ibm.com/history/watson-jeopardy).
- **AlexNet (2012):** [Krizhevsky et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3065386).
- **AlphaGo (2016):** [Google DeepMind project history](https://deepmind.google/research/alphago/).
- **Transformers (2017):** [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762).
- **AlphaFold (2020):** [DeepMind history](https://deepmind.google/science/alphafold/).
- **GPT-3 (2020), ChatGPT (2022):** [Language Models are Few-Shot Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165); [Introducing ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt/).
- **Kira and NewBrain (2025-26):** [Beyond-Chatbots project materials](https://github.com/rmcmurrer81/Beyond-Chatbots). These materials report bounded experimental results, not completed general conversation, embodied personhood or consciousness.

Historical categories overlap; later technologies did not replace all prior approaches. Screenshot credits and differences between tested capabilities and demonstrations are made explicit.

## Author's reflection (2026-10-10)

The new 42-page book includes page 34, *Can a machine become someone?*, credited to Robert McMurrer of Kira Labs. It includes a short, explicitly attributed quotation from Daniel Graystone to Joseph Adama in the *Caprica* pilot, verified against a publicly available transcript. The possibility that machines could someday develop emotions, a moral conscience or consciousness is presented as the author's speculative research motivation, not as established scientific evidence or proof of Maya's subjective experience.

### Pilot-episode dialogue used in the author's reflection

The updated page 34 quotes Daniel Graystone speaking to Joseph Adama while discussing the digital reconstruction of his daughter, Zoe. The quoted dialogue is included with context so readers who have not seen *Caprica* can understand the author's question. Source: [*Caprica* pilot episode transcript](https://www.springfieldspringfield.co.uk/view_episode_scripts.php?episode=s01e01&tv-show=caprica). This fictional characterization of the brain is not presented as neuroscientific fact.
